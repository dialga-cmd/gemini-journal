# LAMPLIGHT

*A design philosophy for the Personal Gemini Journal.*

## The Movement

Lamplight begins with a simple admission: most people journal at night, at the
end of things, when the house is quiet and the screen is the brightest object
in the room. An interface built for that moment must behave like a lamp, not
like a stadium. It casts one warm pool of light onto a deep ink surface, and
everything outside that pool recedes into darkness without ever turning to
pure black, because true black is the absence of a room and this is a room.

The philosophy demands surfaces be built in thin, quiet layers the way ink
soaks into paper: a ground, a raised plane, a held edge. Depth is communicated
by warmth and elevation, never by glow. Nothing shines outward; light is spent
only where attention belongs, on the single accent the way a lamp spends its
filament. That accent is apricot, the exact temperature of a bulb seen through
a shade, and it appears only where the interface invites action: to send a
thought, to begin writing, to recover from an error. Violet exists in the
system as lamplight's shadow side, the faint coolness at the edge of the pool,
reserved for focus, selection, and the trace a person leaves behind in their
own book.

Type is the second lamp. Display letterforms speak in a clear, geometric,
confident voice at sizes that feel almost theatrical against the hush of the
interface, because a blank journal page deserves a greeting with posture. Body
text answers in the same family at whisper volume, set generously for long
reading, since journaling is reading one's own handwriting to oneself. Small
monospaced annotations behave like marginalia: tiny, patient, never competing.

Motion in this system is the behavior of flame, not of machinery. Elements
arrive the way a page settles, a few degrees of travel and a breath of fade,
cascading briefly and then holding perfectly still. Nothing loops forever,
nothing bounces, nothing begs to be watched. Under a preference for reduced
motion the entire room simply holds steady, and that steadiness is treated as
an equal expression of the philosophy rather than a diminished one.

Every corner in the system obeys one geometry: pills where a hand presses,
soft sixteen-degree planes where content rests, and message forms rounded with
a single tucked corner that points to their speaker, the only asymmetry the
philosophy permits because conversation itself is asymmetric. Spacing follows
a strict rhythm of eights widened for contemplation, so that density feels
chosen rather than crowded, and emptiness reads as invitation. The result must
look labored over, meticulously crafted, the product of countless small
judgments by someone who cares about the ten minutes a day a person spends
alone with their own mind.
